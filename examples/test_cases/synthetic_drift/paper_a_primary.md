## Abstract

We evaluated a fictional ceramic-polymer separator coating, NSX-4, in 12 coated and 12 uncoated lithium-ion pouch cells. After 100 charge-discharge cycles, coated cells retained a mean of 88% of initial capacity and controls retained 82%. The observed difference was 6 percentage points. Because this was a small exploratory experiment with substantial cell-to-cell variation, the confidence interval included small or no benefit. These results suggest NSX-4 may improve short-term capacity retention under the tested laboratory protocol, but larger replication studies are required before conclusions about cycle life or other cell chemistries can be drawn.

## Methods

Twenty-four otherwise identical fictional pouch cells were randomized to NSX-4 coating (n=12) or control (n=12). Cells were cycled for 100 cycles at 25 C using the same charging protocol. The primary endpoint was capacity retained at cycle 100 relative to cycle 1.

## Results

- NSX-4 group mean retention: **88%**
- Control group mean retention: **82%**
- Absolute difference: **+6 percentage points**
- Relative difference in retention: approximately **7.3%**
- Illustrative 95% CI for the absolute difference: **-1 to +13 percentage points**

The experiment did **not** measure total battery lifetime, time to failure, safety, performance beyond 100 cycles, human-facing device performance, or other battery chemistries.

## Conclusion

NSX-4 **may** improve capacity retention over 100 cycles under this specific protocol. The result is preliminary and should not be interpreted as demonstrating a general increase in battery lifetime.
